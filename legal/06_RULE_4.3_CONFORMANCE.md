# Rule 4.3 Conformance — why "Designed to conform" is honest

The cover of each pocket edition states **"Designed to conform · Rule 4.3."** This document explains why that claim is truthful, defensible, and accurate — and why we deliberately do NOT say "USGA-approved" or "conforming".

---

## 1. The Governing Rule

The physical size and scale limits for green-reading materials are set forth in **Clarification 4.3a/1, "Limitations on Using Green-Reading Materials"** (USGA &amp; R&amp;A, effective 1 January 2023):

> - *"Any image of a putting green must be limited to a scale of 3/8 inch to 5 yards (1:480) or smaller (the "scale limit")."* — This is a strict **ceiling**; an image scaled smaller (such as 1:600) is fully compliant.
> - *"Any book or other paper containing a map or image of a putting green must not be larger than 4 1/4 inches x 7 inches (the "size limit"), although a "hole location sheet" that displays 9 or more holes on a single sheet of paper may be larger, provided that any image of a single putting green meets the scale limit."*
> - Hand-drawn notes must sit inside a size-limit book and must be written by the player and/or caddie.
> - No magnification may be used.

Under **Rule 4.3a(3)**, pre-printed materials containing information gathered prior to the round are permitted equipment during a stipulated round, provided they observe the scale and size limits above.

### What Rule 4.3 Does and Does Not Regulate
- **Regulated:** The maximum physical scale of a green illustration (≤ 3/8 in : 5 yd), booklet size (≤ 4¼ in × 7 in), absence of magnification, and personal authorship of hand-written notes.
- **NOT Regulated:** Nothing in the Rules of Golf restricts **contour interval, downhill arrow density, or printed slope percentages**.
- **No Conformance List:** Unlike golf balls, drivers, and irons, **neither the USGA nor The R&A tests, certifies, or publishes a conformance list for green books**. Conformance is assessed under the Rules by the Committee in charge of a competition.

## 2. Refutation: The "Dashed Contours" Rumor

A misconception occasionally circulates that Rule 4.3 requires elevation contours on green maps to be drawn with dashed lines. **This is false.**

The phrase *"contours, if produced, should be dashed"* originates not from the Rules of Golf, but from the **American Society for Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing (ASPRS) Positional Accuracy Standards for Digital Geospatial Data** (Edition 2, Annex C, "Low Confidence Areas"). In that remote-sensing standard, dashed contours are recommended specifically where dense vegetation (mangroves, swamps, dense canopy) prevents LiDAR from reliably resolving bare earth.

A search of the USGA and R&A Rules of Golf, Clarification 4.3a/1, the official Green Reading Materials FAQs, and Model Local Rules guidance confirms **zero mention of dashed lines, contour styles, or line weights**. Our contours are drawn solid at a 15 cm interval because airborne LiDAR returns over open golf putting surfaces provide dense, clear ground elevation without vegetation obstruction.

## 3. Model Local Rules G-11 and G-12

Even when a booklet strictly satisfies the 1:480 scale cap and 4¼″ × 7″ size cap, a Committee may enact specific Local Rules under the Official Guide to the Rules of Golf:
- **Model Local Rule G-11:** For elite competitions, a Committee may adopt MLR G-11, requiring players to use **only a single specific yardage book approved by the Committee**. In an event with MLR G-11 active, any other green-reading booklet — no matter how strictly scaled — is prohibited from use for green-reading.
- **Model Local Rule G-12:** A Committee may ban referencing **any** materials to help read the line of play on putting greens.

For this reason, every Lucas Green Book explicitly instructs players:
> *"Designed to fall within the size & scale limits for green-reading materials under Rule 4.3, but conformance is not guaranteed for every hole; and where a Committee has adopted Model Local Rule G-11, green-reading material like this is prohibited whatever its size — confirm with your Committee before competition."*

## 4. How the Pocket Edition is Engineered to Comply

1. **Scale Ceiling Margin:** Green maps are rendered to a maximum scale of **0.36 in : 5 yd** — approximately **4% under the 3/8 in (0.375 in) limit**. This built-in buffer ensures that minor printer tolerances or paper expansion cannot push a green beyond 1:480.
2. **Booklet Dimensions:** Standard pocket cards measure **3.5 × 5.0 inches**, well within the 4.25 × 7.0 inch size ceiling.
3. **Artifact-Level Verification:** Rather than relying on SVG declared dimensions (which can be affected by browser CSS), layout scale is measured under actual print media conditions across every green in the corpus.
4. **Scale & Size Disclosed on Cover:** Following the USGA's guidance for green-reading material publishers, the front cover displays:
   `SCALE 1:480 OR SMALLER · CARD 3.5 × 5.0 IN`
5. **Clear Separation of Practice Editions:** The Coach / Large-Print edition is deliberately designed at a larger scale for readability from arm's length. It is explicitly labeled a **Practice Edition (Not for Competition Use)** and omits the Rule 4.3 cover badge.

## 5. Why the Wording "Designed to Conform" is Honest

- We state **"Designed to conform · Rule 4.3"**, never "USGA Approved" or "Certified Legal". Claiming official approval would be false because golf's governing bodies do not issue pre-approvals for green books.
- The claim is backed by physical measurements of every printed green card against the 1:480 threshold.
- The conditionality of Committee adoption (MLR G-11 / G-12) is transparently disclosed to golfers on the About page of every booklet.
